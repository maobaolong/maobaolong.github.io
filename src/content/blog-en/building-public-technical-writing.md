Many engineers have scattered outputs, but lack a stable place to host these outputs.

I increasingly feel that a long-term operated technical site has at least three layers of value:

## 1. It is your public workspace

Others don’t need to guess your abilities through a resume; they can directly see your judgment process from articles, projects, and thought paths.

## 2. It turns “written once” into “continuous compounding”

Experiences casually shared in groups can be lost in a few weeks; when organized into a blog, they become a citable, searchable, and iterative asset.

## 3. It expands your collaboration radius

When others can understand what you are doing and what you care about, it becomes easier to attract like-minded collaboration opportunities.

Therefore, the goal of this site is not just to look good, but to be long-term maintainable, low-friction for updates, convenient for interaction, and always under your control in your own repository.
